Rork AI Review (2026): Pricing, Limits and What It Really Does

Rork AI turns a chat thread into a mobile app you can put on the App Store. Here is what that covers, what stays your problem, and who should pick something smaller.

The pitch, in the company's own words

The homepage says it plainly: create mobile apps by chatting with AI, ship to the App Store, start making money. Above that sits a rotating wall of famous founders under a be the next app founder headline. Read that as aspiration rather than a customer list, because the faces include people who never touched the product. The one named case study is concrete, though. Rork points at George Lampropoulos, who built an app called Wrestle AI on the platform with, per the write-up, no coding experience, and turned it into an App Store business. One story is not a success rate. Still, it tells you which user the product is designed around: a solo builder with an idea and no engineering team, shipping a consumer app to a phone.

Products behind the nav

Rork is several things stacked together. Rork Max is billed as the most advanced option for building mobile apps, producing Swift apps for Apple devices, with a companion iOS app that lets you build, install and publish on the go. Rork Android covers Kotlin apps, Rork Web covers production apps for the browser, and Rork Games handles native games. Two cloud pieces sit alongside: Rork Backend for realtime data, auth and storage, and Rork AI Cloud, described as multimodal AI for app makers. Two smaller utilities round it out, an AI App Store Reviewer meant to catch App Store rejections before you submit, and Screenshot Studio for store screenshots in one click. Docs, guides and an FAQ live on a separate subdomain.

What the crawl does not tell you about price

There is a pricing link in the main nav and in the footer, and the plan numbers are not on the landing page. I will not guess at them, and you should be suspicious of any review that quotes a figure without a screenshot, because platforms in this category change tiers and credit rules often. Two things are worth checking yourself before signing up. First, how usage is metered, since chat-driven builders typically charge per message, per build or per credit rather than a flat seat price. Second, what happens to an app you have already shipped if you stop paying. Both answers live on the pricing page, and both decide whether the tool is cheap or expensive for your particular project.

Who should look elsewhere

Three cases. If you want a website rather than an app, a mobile builder is the wrong shape entirely. If you need the source in your hands, either for a client or because you plan to hand it to a developer later, check the export story before you build anything substantial. And if your app is mostly a form over a database with real compliance requirements, chat-driven generation gets you to a demo fast and then leaves you negotiating with generated code you did not write. Rork does sell a backend and an App Store review checker, which shows the team knows where people get stuck. Knowing where people get stuck is not the same as the stuck part disappearing.

The parts that matter

Chat in, app out

You describe what you want and iterate in conversation rather than in an editor. The published path runs all the way to the App Store, which is the piece most prompt-to-code tools leave to you.

Three platform tracks

Swift for Apple devices under Rork Max, Kotlin under Rork Android, and a separate web track. Games get their own product. Pick the track before you start, since the output differs by platform.

Store chores, automated

An AI App Store Reviewer flags likely rejection reasons early, and Screenshot Studio generates store screenshots in one click. Small features, but they sit exactly where first-time publishers lose a week.

Optional cloud pieces

Rork Backend covers realtime data, auth and storage, while Rork AI Cloud offers multimodal AI for app makers. Useful, and also the point where your app starts depending on their account.

If you want to try it

  1. Read the pricing page first
    Find out how usage is metered and what happens to a published app if you stop paying. Those two answers change the maths more than any feature on the homepage.
  2. Pick your platform track
    Apple devices, Android or web are separate products with separate output. Choosing after you have built something means redoing work, so decide before the first prompt.
  3. Build something small and real
    One screen, one data source, one thing saved. Small projects expose the edges of a chat builder far faster than an ambitious spec that never reaches the store.

    Describe the same idea at begin.sh, download the Expo project, and see which starting point you would rather hand to a developer six months from now.

Common questions

What is Rork AI in one sentence?

A platform where you build mobile apps by chatting with an AI and then publish them to the app stores. It covers Apple, Android, web and games as separate tracks, and sells backend and multimodal AI services alongside the builder for apps that need data, auth or media.

Does Rork AI cost money?

There is a pricing page linked from the nav and the footer, and the plans are not shown on the landing page, so check it directly. Tools in this category usually meter by credits, builds or messages rather than by seat, which makes your own usage pattern the deciding factor.

Can it publish to the App Store for me?

That is the advertised path. The company also ships an AI App Store Reviewer aimed at catching likely rejections before you submit, and a Screenshot Studio for store assets. You still need your own developer account with Apple, and review outcomes remain Apple's call.

Does it do Android and web too?

Yes, as separate products. Rork Android is described as Kotlin apps for Android devices, Rork Web as production apps for the web, and Rork Games as native games. The iOS track, Rork Max, is the one the homepage leads with and positions as the most advanced.

Is the founder wall on the homepage a list of users?

No. It is a be the next app founder motif cycling through well-known founders, which is a marketing device rather than a claim about customers. The one named customer story on the page is George Lampropoulos and the Wrestle AI app, linked as a blog case study.
